Personalised Map Art Gift Ideas for Every Occasion

There is a particular kind of gift that people keep for decades. It is rarely the most expensive thing in the box. It is the one that says "I remember", and says it so specifically that nobody else could have given it. A map of the street where two people met, the hill where a proposal happened, the finish line of a first marathon, the hospital where a baby arrived: these are gifts built from a place, and a place is something only you and the receiver share.

A custom map poster is one of the simplest ways to turn a place into a gift. You choose a spot, choose a mood, add a line of text, and the result looks as though a designer spent a week on it. This guide is a practical collection of ideas, grouped by occasion. It covers anniversaries, weddings, proposals, new homes, the place two people first met, hometown roots, a baby's arrival, a marathon, a mountain summit and a trip of a lifetime. For each, it suggests what place to choose, what to put on the map, which theme and layout tend to work, and how to word the caption.

Everything here can be done in the Maparte editor in your browser, with a 4K preview so you can see the result before you commit to anything. Maparte provides digital downloads only. We do not print or ship, so we will also talk about the different ways a digital gift can be presented, from a frame you pick yourself to a phone wallpaper delivered by message.

A note on the examples. Where we write sample captions, names or dates, they are illustrations made up to show the idea, not real customers. Please replace them with your own details.

Why a place makes a better gift than a thing

Gifts that are things can feel interchangeable. A candle is a candle. A place is not interchangeable. It carries weight because of what happened there, and a map is one of the few objects that can hold a place without needing a photograph.

There are a few practical reasons a map works so well as a gift.

It is specific without being sentimental in a heavy way. A map is quiet. It does not ask the viewer to cry. It shows the street grid, the river, the park, and the receiver fills in the rest from memory. That is a gentler way to be personal than a loud slogan.

It suits every kind of room. Because there are many themes, a map can be quiet and neutral for a minimalist flat, warm and earthy for a family home, or bold and bright for a studio. We cover themes in detail in our themes and palettes guide.

It works at any scale. A map can be a phone wallpaper, a desktop background, a small A5 card-sized piece or a big wall poster. The layouts guide on this blog shows every shape, and you can use more than one for the same gift.

It does not go out of date. A place stays a place. A map of the city where you got married will mean as much in thirty years as it does today.

How to plan a map gift in five steps

Before the occasions, here is a simple method that works for all of them. It takes about as long as making a cup of tea.

Step 1: choose the exact place

The more exact, the better. "Paris" is a nice poster. "The cafe on the corner where we had our first coffee, in the neighbourhood where we later lived" is a better poster, because it is a particular place. In the editor you can search for a place name or enter exact coordinates, which is useful for spots that do not have a name, such as a bench, a trailhead, a stretch of beach or a hospital entrance.

If you do not know the coordinates, search for the nearest landmark and zoom in until the right street is in the middle of the frame. The poster shows the coordinates of the centre point under the place name, so the numbers on the finished piece are meaningful.

Step 2: decide how wide to look

The zoom level changes the story. A tight zoom shows individual streets and makes the poster about one block or one building. A medium zoom shows a neighbourhood. A wide zoom shows a whole city and its geography. For a first-meeting poster, tight zoom is intimate. For a hometown poster, medium zoom shows the layout of the place you grew up. For a travel memory, wide zoom shows the shape of a coastline or an island.

Step 3: add the personal marks

The editor lets you add markers, so you can point to the exact spot, and style borders and text. For a route, such as a marathon or a trek, you can upload a GPX track, and the route is drawn onto the map. A marker or a route turns a general map into a personal one, because it answers the question, "which spot, exactly?"

Step 4: choose a mood and a shape

Choose a theme that matches the feeling and the room. If you are unsure, our themes guide has a decision method. Then choose a layout that matches the way the gift will be given: A4 Portrait is a good default for a framed piece, a phone layout for a wallpaper, and the caption layouts when you want the words to have their own room.

Step 5: write the words

The text on the poster is short. The place name, the country, and the coordinates are set for you. Everything else is up to you, and the guidance in each occasion below gives example wording. Keep it short. A date or a few words will do more than a paragraph.

Anniversaries and weddings

The wedding day: the venue and the town

A wedding is a day, but it happens in a place. A poster of the wedding venue, the register office, the temple, the garden or the beach is a way of keeping the day in a form that does not fade. It is a natural gift from a friend, from parents, from a sibling and from one partner to the other.

What to put on the map. The venue, centred. If the ceremony and the reception were in different places, a medium zoom that holds both, with a marker on each, tells the story of the day's journey.

Themes. For a classic, formal wedding, Midnight Blue gives a luxurious, gold-on-navy look, and Carrara or Japanese Ink gives a quiet, elegant one. For a soft, romantic feel, Blush, Sunset or Watercolor Wash fit well. For a warm, family-centred wedding, Terracotta is friendly and welcoming.

Layouts. A4 Portrait or A3 Portrait for a frame above a bed or a console table. The Square Caption layout is a lovely fit for a wedding, because the square map above an editable centred caption leaves room for the couple's names and the date, like an invitation.

Wording ideas (examples). Two first names and the date. Or a short line such as "The day we said forever". If the place name is long or unusual, let it sit alone and put the names in the caption.

A thoughtful extra. Ask the couple's families for the coordinates of the venue or the exact name of the garden or hall. Often, the name people use is not the name on a map, and a marker solves the problem.

The first anniversary and beyond

Anniversaries are well suited to the map idea, because an anniversary is an occasion that wants a gift with meaning, and there is a long tradition of giving different materials for different years. You do not need to follow the tradition, but you can use the idea: a different place for each year. The place of the proposal for the first, the place where you first lived together for the fifth, the place where you took the trip you still talk about for the tenth.

What to put on the map. A place that belongs only to the two of you. It might be a restaurant that no longer exists, a street in a city you visited once, or a university campus where you met. If the place has changed, the map still shows the layout of the streets as they are today, so a closed restaurant may not appear, but the street will.

Themes. Ruby for passion, Midnight Blue for permanence, Sunset for warmth. For couples who like quieter tones, Sage and Sandstone are calm and enduring.

Wording ideas (examples). The anniversary date and the number of years. Or just the place, because the poster already carries the coordinates and the couple will know what it means.

She said yes: the proposal

A proposal happens at one specific spot, and that spot is the most precious point on the map. A poster of the proposal location, given after the event, is a keepsake that the couple can display for the rest of their lives. It can also be part of a proposal itself, a surprise gift that shows that you planned the place with care.

What to put on the map. A tight zoom on the spot, with a marker. If it was on a beach, include the coastline. If it was in a city, include the nearby streets. If it was on a mountain, a wider zoom shows the peak and the paths.

Themes. The mood of a proposal is hopeful and romantic. Sunset catches the golden-hour feeling. Ruby is dramatic. Blush and Watercolor Wash are soft. If the proposal happened at night, Midnight Blue or Noir echoes the darkness and the lights.

Layouts. A4 Portrait is a classic. For a gift that is shared digitally, an Instagram Portrait or Square layout works well on a feed, and a phone wallpaper layout lets the couple carry the place with them.

Wording ideas (examples). The date and two words: "She said yes", or "We said yes" if it was a mutual surprise. Keep the place name unchanged, because it is part of the story.

Where we met: the place it all started

Every couple has a story of where it began, and the places in those stories are often unglamorous: a bus stop, a hostel kitchen, a college corridor, a party in a flat. These are the places that make the best posters, because they are known only to the two people involved.

What to put on the map. The meeting point, centred, with a marker. A medium zoom shows how close the two places were. If the couple met in one city and lived in another, a pair of posters, one for each place, makes a striking set.

Themes. Ocean or Blue Water for a place by the sea, Pencil Sketch or Carrara for a minimal, timeless look. Retro Vintage for a story with nostalgia in it. Chalkboard Cafe for a couple who met over coffee.

Layouts. A4 Portrait or A5 Portrait is a good shape for a nightstand or a shelf. For a first-anniversary gift, a phone wallpaper of the place is a small, daily reminder.

Wording ideas (examples). The date you met, or a short phrase such as "Where it all began". If the story has a funny detail, a short caption with a touch of humour can make the gift feel alive.

The first date

A related idea is the place of the first date. A single place can carry a lot of feeling.

A first-date poster can be funny, sweet or both. It makes a good gift for an early anniversary, or a surprise for someone who still remembers what they ordered. In Japanese Ink, the major roads are a single deep red against grey streets, so the poster looks restrained and elegant, which works particularly well for a gift that is meant to be sophisticated rather than sugary.

New homes and first apartments

Our first home: the front door

A new home is one of the most meaningful places in a person's life, and it is a poster that every guest will see. A map of the neighbourhood, centred on the address, is a housewarming gift that is both personal and decorative. It also suits a person who has moved to a new city and wants a reminder of where they live now.

What to put on the map. The address, centred, at a tight to medium zoom. At a tight zoom, you can see the street, the corner and the nearby park. At a medium zoom, you see the neighbourhood and its shape.

Themes. Terracotta feels warm and homely. Blueprint is a witty choice for a new home, since it looks like the plans from which a house begins. Sandstone and Sage suit natural, calm interiors. Carrara and Pencil Sketch fit modern flats.

Layouts. A4 Portrait or A3 Portrait, or the 50 × 70 cm (B2) gallery size if you want something a little larger for a hall or a living room wall. If the gift will be given digitally, the Square Caption layout leaves room for a line like "Our first home" and the move-in date.

Wording ideas (examples). The move-in date, or the address in a short form. A line such as "Home, since" followed by the year. Avoid putting a full address on a poster that will hang in a visible place if privacy matters to you. The coordinates on the poster already identify the location, so think about whether you are comfortable with that. If not, centre the map on the neighbourhood instead of the front door.

A gift for someone who has moved away

Another way to use the idea is to make a poster of the place a person has left. A friend who has moved abroad, a colleague who has retired to another city, a parent who has moved in with their children: each of them might love a map of the neighbourhood they spent years in. It acknowledges the move without being sad about it, and it holds the old streets in a form that can hang in the new home.

Roots, family and new arrivals

Hometown roots: the roads that raised you

For many people, the most emotional place on earth is the small town or neighbourhood where they grew up. It may not be famous, it may have changed beyond recognition, but its layout is fixed in memory: the road to school, the junction where the sweet shop stood, the hill behind the house. A map of a hometown is a gift that suits parents, grandparents, siblings, and anyone who lives far from where they started.

What to put on the map. A medium zoom that takes in the whole neighbourhood or town, with a marker on the family home. If the place is a hill town, a wider view will show the way the roads twist along the slopes, which is often the most beautiful thing about it.

Themes. Hill and forest towns look at home in Sage, Watercolor Wash and Sandstone. A heritage town in a warm climate suits Terracotta or Retro Vintage. For a person who loves a bold, graphic look, Noir or Midnight Blue make a small town look like a serious atlas page.

The Dehradun phone wallpaper in the layouts guide is a good illustration: a mountain town in Sage, with very few roads, shown on a tall canvas where the whitespace is part of the beauty.

Layouts. A4 Portrait for a frame, or a phone wallpaper for someone who lives abroad and wants their home town on their lock screen every time they pick up the phone. For the diaspora in particular, this can be the most-used gift of all, because a lock screen is seen dozens of times a day.

Wording ideas (examples). The name of the town, or a short phrase such as "Where I come from". A parent might prefer the family surname and the years they lived there.

A thoughtful extra. Ask a relative to tell you the family's old address or a landmark. A small marker on the exact house, even if the street has been renamed, can be the detail that makes someone gasp.

Baby arrival: the day you were born

A baby's arrival is a day with a time, a date and a place, and those three things fit very naturally into a map poster. The place may be the hospital, the home where a home birth happened, or the city. A poster for a nursery or a first-birthday gift records where the story began.

What to put on the map. The hospital or the home, centred. A medium zoom includes the surrounding streets, which parents often remember vividly from the drive. Add a marker on the exact building.

Themes. Watercolor Wash, Blush, Sage and Sunset are gentle and suit a nursery. Crayon Art is playful for an older child. For a modern nursery in neutrals, Carrara and Pencil Sketch are calm and go with almost anything.

Layouts. The Square Caption layout is a natural fit, since it leaves a centred space under the map for a name, a date and a time. A4 Portrait or A5 Portrait work for a shelf or a changing table.

Wording ideas (examples). The baby's name, date of birth and time. Or a phrase such as "The day you arrived". If the baby has not been born yet, a map of the place where the parents live is a lovely gift for a baby shower.

A thoughtful extra. A set of posters can grow with a child. A map of the birth place can be followed by one of the first home, one of a family holiday and one of the first school. Each is a chapter in a story, and the matching theme ties them together.

Active lives: marathons, mountains and travel

Marathon and race finishers

Anyone who has trained for a marathon knows that the race is only the last day of a long story. A map poster of the route is a way of honouring all of it. The finish line, the course and the city come together in a single design, and the result is a better memento than a medal in a drawer.

What to put on the map. The route. If you have the GPX file from a watch or a running app, you can upload the track and the route will be drawn on the map. If you do not have it, a marker at the start and the finish tells the story just as well. A medium or wide zoom is best, because a marathon crosses a whole city.

Themes. Dark themes make a coloured route glow: Noir, Midnight Blue, Neon and Aurora are excellent. For a lighter look, Blue Water and Pop Art make the route and the city stand out in a clean, athletic way. If the race is by the sea or a river, Ocean gives it a fresh, open feel.

Layouts. A tall layout such as A3 Portrait or 12 × 18 inch suits a course that runs through the length of a city. A Square layout suits a loop. For a runner who wants to carry the memory, a phone wallpaper is a daily motivator.

Wording ideas (examples). The name of the race, the year and the finishing time. "42.195 km" is the standard marathon distance, and putting it on the poster is a nice touch. For shorter events, the distance and the date will do. Do not guess times or dates; a wrong detail in a keepsake is worse than none.

A thoughtful extra. For a team or a running club, a set of posters of the same course, each with a different finisher's name and time, makes a gift that everyone wants to display.

The mountain summit

Climbing a mountain, completing a trek or reaching a pass is one of those achievements that live in the legs and the memory. The summit has precise coordinates, and a poster of the peak and its surrounding trails is a memorial to the effort.

What to put on the map. The summit, centred, with a marker. A wider zoom than you would use in a city shows the surrounding ridges and paths. If you have a GPX file of the ascent, upload it, and the route winds up the mountain on the poster.

Note that Maparte makes graphic street and place maps. The themes colour land, water, parks, buildings, roads and rail, and they are not topographic charts, so in the mountains you will see the roads and paths that exist in the map data rather than a detailed picture of the terrain. It is a poster to hang, not a map to navigate with.

Themes. Sage and Sandstone suit an earthy, natural feel. Midnight Blue or Blueprint look like survey plans. Noir looks dramatic, like a peak at night. Aurora has a northern, high-latitude feel that suits cold places.

Layouts. A tall layout such as A3 Portrait or 24 × 36 inch gives space for a long route. A wide format such as the Desktop Ultrawide suits a ridge walk.

Wording ideas (examples). The name of the peak, the date, and the altitude if you know it for sure. Only add figures that you have checked, since a wrong altitude on a poster is hard to forgive.

Travel memories: the trip of a lifetime

Travel is the most common reason people look at a map, and a poster of a trip is a gift to yourself as much as to anyone else. A honeymoon, a gap year, a family reunion abroad, a solo journey: each has a place that stands for the whole trip.

What to put on the map. Choose the place that stands for the trip, such as the city where you spent most of your time, the island you could not leave or the town where the best meal happened. A wide zoom shows the geography and gives a sense of scale, and a few markers can mark the spots you remember best.

Themes. The theme can carry the mood of the trip. Ocean or Blue Water for islands and coasts. Terracotta or Retro Vintage for old Mediterranean towns. Neon or Aurora for cities you remember at night. Japanese Ink for a trip to Japan or East Asia, where the minimalist look and red accent feel at home.

Layouts. A4 Portrait or 12 × 18 inch for a framed piece. For a shared travel group, an Instagram Square or Portrait layout lets you post the place with the group's memory attached.

Wording ideas (examples). The year, the trip's name or a single sentence such as "The summer we got lost in Lisbon". A sense of humour is welcome here.

A thoughtful extra. A gallery wall of several small posters, each in the same theme, makes a set of travel memories that looks planned and consistent. Pick the same theme and layout, and change only the place.

Writing the words: caption ideas for every occasion

The words on a map poster are small, which is exactly why they matter. A few well-chosen words carry more weight than a paragraph, because they sit beside a place and borrow its meaning. Here are ideas, all of them examples to adapt, grouped by feeling.

Plain and factual

Plain captions are often the strongest. A date, a name and nothing else. "14 February 2020." "Anna and Rahul." "Our first flat." The poster already says where; the caption says when or who. If you are unsure, choose a plain caption, because it never feels forced and it ages well.

Short phrases

A short phrase works when the place has a story. "Where it all began." "The road home." "The summer we got lost." "Still here." The best phrases are the ones the recipient could have said themselves. If you have to explain a phrase, it is too clever for a poster.

Inside jokes

A shared joke can turn a poster into a private language. If a couple always argued about which way the cafe door opened, the caption can say "It opens inwards." Nobody else will understand and that is the point. Be careful to choose something that will still make you smile in ten years, and avoid anything that depends on a passing trend.

Coordinates as the caption

The coordinates are already part of the design, so for some gifts they are all the text you need. A poster that simply shows a place name, a country and a pair of numbers has a quiet, scientific elegance. For a summit, a grave-side family place or a spot with deep meaning, letting the numbers speak can be moving.

What to leave out

Leave out anything you are not certain about, such as dates, times, altitudes or finishing positions. Leave out long quotations, because small text on a poster is hard to read. And leave out private information, such as a full address, if the poster will hang where visitors can see it.

Building a set: when one poster is not enough

A single poster is a gift. A set is a story. If the occasion calls for something more substantial, consider one of these ideas.

The then-and-now pair. Two posters of the same couple's two homes, the first flat and the current house, in the same theme. The pairing shows how far they have come.

The family map wall. A grandparent's home town, a parent's home town and the family's current city, in three posters with a shared theme and layout, hung side by side. It becomes a map of three generations.

The year-by-year anniversary. One poster each year, always the same layout and theme, always a different place. After ten years, the wall tells the story of the marriage.

The race series. For a runner, a poster for each marathon, in a theme that gets bolder or more colourful as the series grows. Using the same layout makes the set look curated.

The trip diptych. A wide-zoom poster of the region and a tight-zoom poster of the favourite spot, in the same theme. One tells where, the other tells exactly where.

When you build a set, keep three things constant: the theme, the layout and the position of the text. Change only the place and the caption. That consistency is what makes separate pieces read as one collection, and it also makes your own decisions easier, since you have only one variable to think about.

A simple checklist before you finish

Before you decide that your design is done, run through this short list.

  1. Is the place exactly the one you mean, with the marker in the right spot?
  2. Is the zoom level right for the story: a block, a neighbourhood or a whole city?
  3. Does the theme suit the recipient's taste and room, not just yours?
  4. Does the layout suit the way you will give it: a frame, a wallpaper or a screen?
  5. Is every date, name and figure correct?
  6. Have you looked at it at both small and large size in the 4K preview?
  7. Have you thought about how the recipient will receive it, and added a few words of your own?

If every answer is yes, the gift is ready.

How to give a digital gift well

Because Maparte provides digital files only, the way you present the gift is part of the gift. Here are some approaches that work.

Frame it yourself

If the recipient would like a framed poster, you can take the file to a printer or framing shop of your choice. For that route, choose one of the paper-size layouts, such as A4, A3, 12 × 18 inch, 24 × 36 inch or the 50 × 70 cm gallery size, so the proportions match the frames you plan to use. Printing and framing are separate from Maparte, so check the print shop's file requirements and the paper or finish before you order.

Give it as a wallpaper

A phone or desktop wallpaper is delivered instantly and it is used every day. Choose the layout that matches the recipient's device, and send the file by message with a short note. For a long-distance relationship or a friend abroad, this can be more meaningful than something that has to be posted across the world, because it lands in the moment.

Present it on a screen

If you are visiting, show the poster on your phone or a tablet. It can be part of a surprise, such as a birthday dinner where the final course is a screen showing the map and the story behind it.

Pair it with a story

The best gift cards for a map poster say why the place was chosen. A few sentences about the day you remember is enough. If you are giving it in person, tell the story out loud.

Make a pair or a set

Two places for two people, three places for three children, or five posters for five years of anniversaries: sets are more memorable than singles, and they make the second poster easy to choose.

Common mistakes when making a map gift

Choosing a place that is too general. "London" is a poster; "the road behind the flat where we lived in 2019" is a gift. Use the search, zoom and marker tools to be specific.

Cramming in too much text. The coordinates and the place name already tell a story. A short date or a short phrase is enough.

Forgetting privacy. A poster that shows a home address will be seen by every visitor. Consider centring on the neighbourhood instead.

Choosing a theme the recipient will not like. Check their taste before you pick. If you are unsure, a calm, neutral theme such as Carrara, Pencil Sketch or Sandstone is rarely a mistake.

Leaving it to the last minute. The design takes only minutes, but if you plan to print and frame it, allow time for the printer and the framer, because you may need to arrange both yourself.

Using details you have not checked. Dates, times and figures printed on a keepsake should be double-checked. Ask a family member, check a certificate or look at your race result.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make a map poster of any place?

You can search for a place name or enter coordinates, and the editor lets you adjust the view. The map data comes from OpenStreetMap, so places that are well mapped there look best.

Can I try it before I download?

Yes. The editor at maparte.com shows a 4K preview, so you can design your poster and see it in detail before you decide anything else.

Does Maparte print and send the poster to my recipient?

No. Maparte provides digital downloads only and does not print or ship. You can give the file as a wallpaper, show it on a screen, or take it to a printer of your choice.

Can I add my running or hiking route?

Yes. The editor supports GPX tracks, so you can upload a route from a watch or an app and see it on the map. You can also add markers for particular spots.

Can I put names and dates on the poster?

Yes. You can style the text, and the caption layouts leave dedicated room for a name or a date. Keep it short for the cleanest look.

How do I choose between several themes?

Use the comparison routine in our themes guide: fix the place and layout, compare at small and large size, and check the text. If you are unsure, choose a theme that matches the recipient's home or favourite colours.

What is the best gift for a long-distance friend or family member?

A phone wallpaper of a shared place is delivered instantly and seen every day, and it is quick to send. A pair of matching posters of two different places is a thoughtful second idea.

What if the place has changed or no longer exists?

A map shows the streets and areas as they are today. If a building has gone, the street is still there, so you can place a marker where it used to be and let the caption tell the story.

Start with one place

Think of a place that matters to someone you care about. Open maparte.com, search for it, try two themes and look at the 4K preview. If it makes you smile, it will probably make them smile too.
